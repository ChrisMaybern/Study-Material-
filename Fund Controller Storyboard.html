01 Daily

The Ordinary Day — Monitoring, Journal Entries & Requests

Any Tuesday, mid-quarter
☀️
Most days are not quarter-end. Sarah's daily life is dominated by journal entry bookkeeping, answering LP questions, and fielding requests from the deal team. The GL is her system of record — but it was designed for accounting, not for answering "how much carry has accrued?" That question lives somewhere else.
8:45 AM Sarah Opens Allvue. Checks yesterday's journal entries.
Sarah opens Allvue — Northbrook's GL — and checks the prior day's activity. A capital contribution came in from LP #12 (Statewide Pension Fund) yesterday. Michael posted the journal entry: debit cash, credit capital contribution. Standard.

She spots a management fee accrual that needs to be reviewed before month-end. She makes a note but doesn't touch it yet — that's for Michael this afternoon.
What the GL does well

Every transaction is recorded cleanly. The capital account balances are correct at the entity level. Allvue knows that $3.2M came in. It does not know — and was never designed to know — what that means for LP #12's waterfall position or carry accrual.

10:15 AM Michael Deal team asks: "If Deal IV exits at $180M, what's the carry?"
The CFO forwards an email from the deal team: they're modeling a potential exit for Portfolio Company IV and want to know what carried interest would accrue to the GP if the exit closes at $180M.

Michael opens the waterfall model — a 1,200-row Excel file Sarah built three years ago. He finds the "Deal IV" tab. Updates the exit proceeds input cell (C14). Checks that the European waterfall formula is still reading the right fund-level capital contribution total. It is, but only because Michael knows to look.
The problem

The waterfall Excel is not connected to Allvue. Michael manually typed in the capital contribution total last quarter. If LP #12's contribution yesterday changes the aggregate called capital, the model is already stale — and neither Michael nor Sarah knows it yet.

Allspring Endowment (LP #7) sends a routine request: they want their current capital account balance, ITD distributions, and accrued hypothetical carry for their internal reporting.

The capital account balance is in Allvue — Sarah can pull that. But the LP-level carry accrual is not in Allvue. She goes back to the waterfall Excel, finds LP #7's commitment tab, and manually traces through the waterfall tiers to produce a number. Then she emails it.

This takes 45 minutes. It should take 2.
The gap

The GL holds accounting data at the entity level. LP-level economics — who has earned what, when, and based on what calculation — live nowhere except Sarah's Excel. There is no system of record for this. If Sarah leaves, this knowledge leaves with her.

02 Monthly

Month-End Close — The GL Books, Fees Accrue, Ops Gets Involved

Last 3 business days of the month
📅
Monthly close is manageable — it's mainly management fee accruals, expense reconciliations, and making sure Allvue balances. The admin isn't heavily involved yet. But it's the month-end that sets up the pain of quarter-end: any entry posted in the GL this month will need to be reconciled to the admin's numbers in about 8 weeks.

03 Quarterly

Quarter-End Close — Where Everything Gets Hard

Weeks 1–5 after quarter end
🔥
This is where Sarah and Michael earn their salary — and where the fragility of the status quo becomes visible. Five moving pieces have to land simultaneously: investment valuations from the deal team, NAV calculation, hypothetical waterfall, fund performance metrics, and LP statements. Everything depends on everything else. And most of it runs through one Excel file that no one else fully understands.
Week 1 · Day 2 Sarah Valuation committee sets FMVs. Sarah has to translate them to Unrealized G/L.
The deal partners send over updated Fair Market Values for each unrealized portfolio company. Sarah's job: convert each FMV into an Unrealized Gain/Loss — which means calculating the change from the prior quarter's FMV, adjusted for any additional cost basis changes.

She does this in a separate Excel tab. For each of Northbrook's 8 portfolio companies, she calculates: FMV today − prior FMV − new investment = Unrealized G/L this quarter. Then she manually posts each one as a PnL journal entry in Allvue.

This calculation must happen before NAV can be calculated, before the hypo waterfall can run, and before Citco can begin producing statements. Everything is downstream of this.

Week 1 · Day 4 Michael NAV calculation — the number every LP cares about
Michael runs the NAV calculation. He pulls the trial balance from Allvue — total capital contributions, total distributions, total PnL (realized + unrealized) — and builds a NAV Walk in Excel.

Prior NAV + capital contributions − distributions + PnL = Current NAV (pre-carry).

Then he calculates hypothetical carry. This requires running the waterfall — that same 1,200-row Excel file — against the current pre-carry NAV to estimate what the GP would be owed if the fund liquidated today. Post-carry NAV = the number LPs actually care about for their quarterly statements.

He emails Sarah a summary. She reviews it. They both agree the numbers look right. But neither can easily explain to an auditor exactly how each component was derived.

Week 2 · Day 1 Admin (Citco) Citco sends their preliminary NAV. It's $2.1M different from Sarah's.
Amanda at Citco emails over their preliminary NAV: $292,740,000. Sarah's number was $294,850,000. A $2.1M discrepancy.

Sarah is not panicked — this happens every quarter on the first pass. But now begins the reconciliation process. She and Michael spend the next two days going line by line, comparing:
$800K diff Citco accrued management fees using a different day count convention (365 vs 360). Neither is wrong — just different.
$1.1M diff Citco used a different FMV for Portfolio Company VI — they hadn't received the updated valuation committee memo yet.
$200K diff Timing — a distribution wire hit Citco's books one day after Sarah's cutoff. Expected, now confirmed.
The deeper problem

Citco and the GP are running parallel calculations with different assumptions, different data, and no shared source of truth for the waterfall math. This reconciliation is normal — but "normal" is the problem. It's two to three days of Sarah and Michael's time every quarter, every fund.

Week 3 · Day 2 Amanda (Citco) LP statements ready — but carry number came from Sarah's email, not a system
After reconciliation is complete, Sarah emails Amanda the agreed-upon numbers: final NAV, hypo carry accrual per LP, and the fund-level carry breakdown. Amanda plugs these into Citco's statement system and produces LP capital account statements for all 47 investors.

The carry number that lands in every LP's statement is a number that came from a 1,200-row Excel file, emailed as an attachment, entered manually into Citco's system by Amanda.

If Sarah had made a formula error in the waterfall model — say, the catch-up rate was referencing the wrong cell — that error is now in 47 LP statements. It has been reviewed by Sarah, spot-checked by Michael, and accepted by Amanda. No one ran an independent check.

Week 5 · Ongoing Sarah Auditor asks: "Can you show me the waterfall calculation and how it ties to your GL?"
Ernst & Young is doing the annual audit. The audit manager sends a request: please provide documentation of the waterfall calculation methodology, the assumptions used, and how the carry figure ties to the trial balance.

Sarah opens the Excel model and starts building a memo. She has to manually trace every hardcoded cell, explain every formula assumption, and construct a bridge from her waterfall output back to the Allvue trial balance.

This takes three days. Every year. The auditors accept it. But every year, the partner on the engagement writes a management letter comment: "The fund's waterfall calculations rely on a manual Excel model without automated controls. We recommend implementing a system-based solution."

David (CFO) reads it. Files it. Moves on. Until the year he doesn't.
The audit letter is your sales opening

When a prospect says "our auditors have commented on our Excel models" — that's the trigger. That management letter is them telling you they already know the problem. The question is whether they've found the urgency to do something about it.

What auditors want

Proof that the carry number in the statements is derived from and consistent with the GL books. Currently: manual bridge, 3-day exercise.

04 Critical Moment

The Breaking Point — When the Status Quo Fails

Any quarter. Every fund has this eventually.
⚡
The status quo works — until it doesn't. These are the scenarios that create urgency. They're not hypotheticals. Every fund controller you'll speak to has either experienced one of these or is quietly terrified they will.
Michael Michael leaves the firm

Michael built three tabs of the waterfall model over two years. He's the only person who can explain the catch-up formula logic. He leaves in two weeks. Sarah is now sole owner of a model she partially understands and cannot safely hand to a new hire without six months of knowledge transfer. The knowledge walked out the door.

The question to ask

"Who runs the waterfall model today — and what happens to that process if that person isn't available?"

Sarah An LP disputes their carry accrual

A large pension fund LP comes back after receiving their statement and questions the $4.2M carry accrual on their commitment. Sarah has to go back to Excel version 14, trace through the catch-up tier math, and explain why that number is correct. She finds a formula reference error. It's small — $40K — but it's real. Restatement discussion begins.

The question to ask

"Has there ever been a moment where you weren't fully confident the numbers were right before they went out?"

Fund Growth Northbrook closes Fund IV — new vehicle, same Excel

Northbrook raises Fund IV, a new vehicle with slightly different waterfall terms — a tiered carry structure with an IRR hurdle instead of a pref. Sarah copies the Fund III Excel and starts modifying. Eight months later, she's not sure if the Fund IV model is calculating the IRR hurdle correctly. The admin sends a different number. Nobody is certain who is right.

The question to ask

"When you add a new vehicle, does someone have to go rebuild the Excel, or does it just extend?"

Citco Admin and GP carry numbers diverge by $5M

On the Q4 close — the biggest quarter of the year with two portfolio company distributions — Citco's carry number is $5.2M lower than Sarah's. It's not a reconcilable timing difference. Citco used a different interpretation of the LPA's catch-up provision. Both interpretations are defensible. Lawyers get involved. LP statements are delayed by 3 weeks. David (CFO) is not pleased.

The question to ask

"How does your team confirm that your waterfall calculation is consistent with what your admin is producing?"

Where Maybern enters the story

Not instead of the GL. Not instead of the admin. In the gap between them — owning the calculation layer that has always lived in Excel and in Sarah's head.

What Maybern replaces
The 1,200-row Excel waterfall model. The manual data bridge from GL to carry calculation. The emailed PDF that becomes the LP statement input. The 3-day audit bridge exercise.
What Maybern doesn't replace
The GL (Allvue, Intacct, NetSuite). The admin (Citco, SS&C, Apex). Sarah and Michael's judgment. The LPA terms. The audit itself. Maybern sits between these — it doesn't replace them.
The one-line pitch
"Your GL records what happened. Your admin reports it. Maybern is where you prove it — before anyone asks."
