Platform Overview & Navigation
Orient yourself in the Maybern platform before diving into specific calculations. This guide is designed so you can have the platform open side-by-side and follow along step by step.
🎯 Positioning Framework
Never lead with features. Before showing any screen, anchor the conversation in the prospect's world: "The core problem we solve is that GPS deserve in-house control of their data and core workflows." Maybern is a system of work, not just a system of record — every calculation step, every formula, and every piece of business logic is captured and auditable.
"I would not want my fund admin telling me what technologies to use. They just don't get it. They're two decades behind." — Ross Mechanic, opening frame in demos

3
Recommended Demo Flow Order
The optimal sequence for showing calculations
▼

Based on analysis of 40+ demo recordings, this sequence produces the strongest engagement. Adapt to your prospect's pain points — but this is the proven default.

  • 1. Commitments — Orient the LP base (2-3 min)
  • 2. Data Model — Show the transaction register briefly (1 min)
  • 3. Capital Calls — Book a call, show allocation, generate notices (5-7 min)
  • 4. Fees — Show configuration depth, fee breaks, offsets (4-5 min)
  • 5. Waterfall — Run cash waterfall, show tier-by-tier audit trail (7-10 min)
  • 6. NAV Roll — Input P&L, show ending NAV flow (3-4 min)
  • 7. Performance — Gross/net IRR, investor-level lookthrough (3-4 min)
  • 8. Pivots/Reports — Show custom data cuts (2-3 min)
  • 9. Scenario Modeling — Forward-looking vision piece (3-5 min)
  • 10. AI Assistant — "Wow" moment at the end (2-3 min)
⚠️
Stage 1 calls are diagnostic, not a demo. If this is your first meeting with a prospect, you should NOT be showing product. Stage 1 earns the right to diagnose. It creates doubt around the long-term scalability and defensibility of the current logic layer. Only demo when you've earned it.
Module 1
LP Commitments & Investor Base
Orient the prospect to their investor base in Maybern. This is always your entry point after clicking into a fund family — it grounds everything that follows.
🎯 How to Position This
"Let me start by showing you where your investors would live in the system. What Maybern becomes is a single pane of glass across your entire investor base — every commitment, every class, every side letter term. You'll be able to filter, drill down, and see any investor's full history across all your funds in a few clicks. No more pulling from the admin."
"For those ad-hoc investor requests your IR team needs to handle, they can now self-serve rather than waiting for your admin to provide reports." — Demo framing used by Gabi with Brighton Park
1
Navigate to Commitments
View the full LP base for a fund family
▼
Fund Family → Commitments
👆

In the left sidebar, click Commitments. This shows a summary table of all investors in the fund family with their commitment amounts, unfunded balances, contributed capital, distributed capital, and subsequent close information.

👁️

What you'll see: A table with all investors listed. You can aggregate by Investor Class to show reduced fee vs. standard investors. Each row shows commitment, unfunded, contributed, distributed, and close date.

💡
Demo move: Ask the prospect: "How many investors are across your funds?" Then say: "All of those investors would live here — and as we get into calls, distributions, and fees, you'll see how everything flows from this investor base. No more reconciling investor lists across spreadsheets."
2
Drill Into an Individual Investor
Show the investor-level lookthrough
▼
👆

Click on any investor name (e.g., Redmont Capital). This opens their investor detail view showing all capital activity, transaction history, performance metrics, and the ability to toggle by fund and by investment.

🔍

Key things to show:

  • Transaction codes and dates for all capital activity
  • Toggle by fund within the fund family
  • Toggle by investment (if applicable)
  • Performance metrics (gross/net IRR) at investor level
  • Cross-fund investor lookthrough (if in multiple vehicles)
🧠
Why this matters: This is where Maybern becomes the "investment book of record." Every call, distribution, fee, and carry allocation that touches this investor is visible in one place — across all funds. Prospects with co-investment vehicles consistently react strongly to this view.
3
Show Investor Classes & Side Letter Terms
Demonstrate how economic terms are encoded per investor group
▼
Fund Family → Configuration → Classes
👆

Navigate to Configuration → Classes in the left sidebar. This shows all investor classes with their specific economics — fee rates, fee basis, carry rates, waterfall overrides — compared side-by-side against the fund family default.

👁️

What you'll see: A comparison view showing the class-specific economics (left) vs. fund family defaults (right). Differences are highlighted — different fee rates, different bases, different waterfall structures. Each class maps to a group of investors who share the same side letter arrangements.

💡
Demo move: "Once you get to Fund III and that sales team wants to close new commitments with a fee break, you set up a new class and those investors inherit the economics. No manual tracking, no spreadsheet versioning. It's all governed in the system."
Module 2
Capital Calls
Walk through the end-to-end capital call workflow — from initiating a call to generating investor notices. This is one of the most impactful demo sequences.
🎯 How to Position This
"Let me show you what it would look like to actually book a capital call in Maybern. This is where it goes from being a data viewer to a system of work. You'll call for management fees — which are already calculated in the system — call for investments, and in a few clicks the system allocates across your LP base, updates unfunded commitments, updates your borrowing base, and generates notices. Everything stays in sync."
"With this approach, you can prepare everything in Maybern first, then simply have your admin execute — eliminating the back-and-forth that's currently causing delays." — Brighton Park demo
1
Navigate to Capital Activity
View historical calls and prepare a new one
▼
Fund Family → Capital Activity
👆

Click Capital Activity in the left sidebar. This shows a chronological history of all capital calls and distributions for the fund family.

👁️

What you'll see: A timeline of capital events — each showing the date, type (call or distribution), total amount, and the components called for (management fees, investments, expenses, etc.).

💡
Before clicking "Add Call": Ask the prospect: "When you call capital, what do you typically call for? Management fees? Investments? Expenses?" This makes the next step feel tailored to them.
2
Create a Capital Call
Add components, set the date, and generate allocations
▼
👆

Click the + Add Call button at the top of the Capital Activity page. Select the call date and then add components:

  • Management Fees — Pulls automatically from the Fees module (already calculated)
  • Investments — Enter the investment amount being called
  • Partnership Expenses — Optional: add any fund-level expenses
  • Equalization Interest — Auto-calculated for subsequent closings
⚡

Key moment: When you add management fees, highlight that Maybern already has them calculated. Say: "Notice I didn't need to enter a fee amount — the system already knows the fees for this quarter based on the configuration we set up. It pulls them automatically."

⚠️
Common prospect question: "Are investments typically pro rata, or are there certain complications from an allocation perspective?" Be ready to show the allocation engine — unfunded commitment percentage vs. custom allocation rules.
3
Review Investor Breakdown & Allocation Schedule
Show the per-investor allocation before finalizing
▼
👆

After adding components, click Investor Breakdown (or navigate to the allocation view). This shows how the call is allocated across all investors based on the fund's allocation rules.

👁️

What you'll see: A table showing each investor's allocation percentage, their share of each component (fees, investments, expenses), and total call amount. The allocation percentages are based on the fund's configured rules (typically unfunded commitment %).

⚡

Key selling moment: "Everything's integrated — when you call capital, your unfunded gets updated, your borrowing base gets updated, your fee basis gets updated. That's the benefit versus needing to update different Excel workbooks in multiple files."

4
Generate Capital Call Notices
Produce downloadable investor notices from the call
▼
👆

After finalizing the call, click Generate Notices. Select which investors to generate notices for (or all). The system produces customizable PDF notices for each investor with their specific allocation amounts.

📄

Show a sample notice: Click on any investor's notice to download and show. These are fully customizable templates — the data (amounts, dates, bank wiring instructions) is dynamic from the system.

💡
Bridge to value: "This entire process — call, allocate, generate notices — took us about 2 minutes. For most of our clients, this replaced a multi-day process with their administrator. One client told us their capital calls went from 3 days to same-day execution."
Module 4
Management Fee Configuration & Calculation
Show how Maybern encodes the exact fee economics from the LPA — basis, cadence, day count, breaks, offsets, waivers, and the commitment-to-invested switch.
🎯 How to Position This
"Management fees sound simple — but every fund has nuances. Different bases per investor class, switches from committed to invested capital at successor fund close, offsets for placement fees, fee waivers with lifetime caps. When these live in Excel, every quarter is a manual exercise. In Maybern, you configure the rules once, and the system calculates correctly every quarter going forward. The fees then pull directly into your capital calls."
"It shouldn't be as hard of a calc as it is for the fund admin. But we still find errors." — Sageview prospect during Initial Demo, describing exactly why this matters
1
View the Fee Summary
See calculated fees with gross-to-net bridge
▼
Fund Family → Fees
👆

Click Fees in the left sidebar. This shows the fee calculation results — the gross-to-net bridge including: gross fees, fee breaks for reduced fee investor classes, offsets (placement fees, monitoring fees), and the net fee amount.

👁️

What you'll see: A summary showing gross management fees, broken down by the fee basis calculation, then subtracting breaks and offsets to arrive at net fees. You'll see this by quarter and by investor.

💡
Discovery questions to ask: "Do you have offsets?" "Placement fees? Monitoring fees?" "Do you have a fee waiver? Cashless contributions?" Each "yes" is another data point that shows why this belongs in a system, not Excel.
2
Show Fee Configuration Depth
Navigate to the LPA config to show the engine under the hood
▼
Fund Family → Configuration → Profile (Fees section)
👆

Navigate to Configuration → Profile and scroll to the Fees section (or navigate directly to the fee configuration within LPA Config). Show the parameters:

  • Cadence — quarterly, semi-annual, annual
  • Day count methodology — actual/365, 30/360, etc.
  • Advance vs. arrears — when fees are calculated relative to the period
  • Basis timing — locked at start, end, or average of period
  • True-up methodology — does basis change mid-quarter trigger a true-up?
  • Fee basis switch — commitment to invested capital at successor fund close
🧠
This is the "can it handle us?" test. When prospects see this level of configurability, they realize Maybern has actually read their LPA. The fee basis switch from committed to invested capital (which nearly every PE fund has) is a particularly strong moment — ask: "When do your fees switch from committed to invested basis?" and then show them the configuration handles it.
3
Book Fees for the Next Quarter
Show this is a system of work, not just a summary tool
▼
Fund Family → Fees
👆

Back in the Fees module, show how to book management fees for the next quarter. The system calculates the fee based on all configured parameters and writes the transaction into the system. Emphasize: "It's not just a summary tool — you actually book fees here."

⚡

Connect the dots: "These fees we just booked? They'll automatically pull into your next capital call. When we go back to Capital Activity and add a new call, the management fee component is already there, already calculated, already allocated. That's the power of an integrated system."

Module 5
Waterfall Calculations
The crown jewel of the demo. Show how Maybern configures, runs, and audits distribution waterfalls — the most complex and highest-stakes calculation in fund management.
🎯 How to Position This
"This is really the heart of Maybern. Your waterfall is the most consequential calculation in your fund — it determines how much your LPs get back, how much carry your partners earn. Right now, this lives in a 15-tab Excel workbook that takes hours or days to update, is impossible to audit quickly, and creates genuine risk when the person who built it leaves. In Maybern, we configure your exact waterfall tiers, run them through our proprietary calculation engine, and show you every single step of the math. Like a good math professor — we show all our work."
"You basically input the fund-level components and it spits out your LP-level components based on the rules you set." — Sageview prospect, confirming they understood the value immediately
1
View the Waterfall Configuration
Show how your exact LPA waterfall tiers are encoded
▼
Fund Family → Configuration → Waterfall
👆

Navigate to Configuration → Waterfall. This shows the waterfall tier structure as configured from the LPA. You'll see each tier — ROC, pref, GP catch-up, carried interest split — with the calculation methodology displayed.

👁️

What you'll see: Tier-by-tier waterfall structure showing: tier name, calculation type (MXL formula), whether it's deal-by-deal or whole-fund, the carry partner, and the LP/GP split at each tier. Each tier links to its underlying calculation formula.

💡
Demo move: Ask the prospect their waterfall type first: "Are you deal-by-deal or European?" Then navigate to the configuration and point: "You can see here — your ROC tier, your pref at 8%, your GP catch-up, your final carry split. This is your waterfall, encoded in software, running through our calculation engine instead of an Excel macro."
2
Run a Distribution Waterfall
Input realized gains and execute the waterfall in real time
▼
Fund Family → Capital Activity → Add Distribution
👆

Navigate to Capital Activity and click + Add Distribution. Enter the distribution components — return of capital, realized gain, etc. Then click Run Waterfall.

⚡

This is the "wow" moment. In a few clicks, the system runs the full waterfall through every tier, allocates across all investors based on their commitment-level economics, and produces the complete distribution breakdown. Say: "What just happened in 10 seconds would take your team hours in Excel."

👆

Click View Waterfall to see the visualization. You'll see a summary showing cash flowing through: pref → return of capital → GP catch-up → carry split. The carried interest amount is highlighted — this is what the GP earns.

3
Audit the Waterfall — "Show Your Work"
Drill into tier-by-tier calculations with full transparency
▼
👆

From the waterfall view, click into any tier (e.g., the pref calculation) to see the underlying math. This shows every input — every cash flow, every date, every rate — that produced the output. The system shows all its work.

👁️

What you'll see: The detailed pref calculation showing: all contributing cash flows, the compounding methodology, the dates used, the rate applied, and the resulting pref amount. Every number links back to a source transaction.

💡
Positioning line: "If your auditors were to come in and ask questions about any of these numbers — this is all exportable. We include every input so they can recalc the output right there. It's designed for auditing teams."
🔄

Show class-level flexibility: Toggle the waterfall view to show different investor classes. "You can view this for any individual investor class, any individual fund. The waterfall runs on a commitment-by-commitment, bottoms-up basis."

⚠️
Technical demo prep: For tailored demos, know the prospect's specific waterfall nuances before walking in — deferred carry, accelerated paydown, switching denominators, opt-out investors. These are the "stress tests" that close deals. Carmel, Arctos, Sterling, and WestCap all deep-dived on exact waterfall mechanics.

In Maybern, all of that data already lives in one place. You input your P&L buckets, and the system produces your ending NAV in a few clicks."

Navigate to Financial Reporting
Input P&L and generate the NAV roll
▼
Fund Family → Financial Reporting
👆
